Using AI Responsibly

The most important lesson in the course. AI is genuinely useful — and confidently wrong often enough that you need a habit of checking.

Hallucination: the one word to remember

When an AI states something false with full confidence — a fake statistic, a book that doesn't exist, a citation that looks real but isn't — that's called a hallucination. It's not lying on purpose; it's generating plausible-sounding text, and "plausible" isn't the same as "true."

💡 The habit that fixes most of this

For anything that matters — a fact you'll repeat, a number you'll use, a quote you'll cite — verify it against a real source before you rely on it. Treat AI output as a strong first draft, not a finished answer.

A short checklist

🔍

Verify facts

Especially numbers, quotes, and citations — search for the source before repeating it.

🔒

Guard privacy

Don't paste passwords, ID numbers, medical info, or anyone else's personal data.

⚖️

Know it's not advice

For legal, medical, or financial decisions, use AI to understand options — not as the final word.

🧭

Keep judgment yours

AI is a tool for thinking faster, not a replacement for thinking. The decision — and responsibility — stays with you.
