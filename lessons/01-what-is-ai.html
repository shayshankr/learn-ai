What is AI, actually?

"AI" gets used for everything from a spam filter to a chatbot that writes poetry. Let's untangle the terms so the rest of the course makes sense.

Three words people mix up

You'll hear AI, machine learning, and LLM used almost interchangeably. They're not the same thing — each one is a smaller circle inside the last.

Artificial Intelligence is the whole field: any computer system that does something we'd normally say needs "intelligence" — even a simple chess program from the 1980s counts.

Machine Learning is a specific approach to AI: instead of a programmer writing exact rules, the system learns patterns from data. A spam filter that improves as it sees more email is machine learning.

LLMs (Large Language Models) are a type of machine learning model trained on huge amounts of text, so they get very good at predicting what word should come next. That simple trick, at massive scale, is what powers ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini.

💡 The one-line version

An LLM doesn't "know" facts the way a database does — it's really good at generating text that sounds like a plausible continuation of your prompt. That's both its superpower and its biggest limitation (more on that in Lesson 8).
